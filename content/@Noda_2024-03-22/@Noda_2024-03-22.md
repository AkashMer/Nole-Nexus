# What are Representational Maps?
![[representational_maps.svg]]
On the surface, a representational map is an identification map of responses to stimuli. But at the core, representational maps are really about similarity of varied stimuli in a response space supplementing the topographical space to reveal complex inferences.
I used a detective's murder board with strings connecting similarities onto an informative topological space(ie. the board) as a metaphor here. Before I read this paper I was under the impression that maps in the brain were purely topologically organized.

# How do representational maps connect to single neuron tuning curves?
![[decorrelation_correlation_balance.svg]]
On the surface, this is about single neuron tuning properties. But at the core, the relational structure of representational maps collapses without correlated tuning curves and balance of diversity and redundancy in tuning properties.
I used a conductor-less orchestra to capture this insight from the paper since an orchestra requires a balance of diversity and repeated instruments to produce a symphony. The naive assumption that a conductor-less orchestra separated into instrument groups would produce a symphony collapses when correlation among instruments at the border of each groups is considered.

# How to estimate representational maps?
![[estimation_pipeline.svg]]
On the surface, estimating a representational map is an algorithm. But at the core, it is really about informed choices and pitfalls across the whole process culminating into a validated and meaningful representational map.
The validation of estimated representational maps is the most crucial part.

> [!synthesis]
> **Claim**: Representational maps are structures of relational similarity in abstract neural activity space, where the same stimulus set is simultaneously embedded in multiple coexisting maps at different hierarchical levels, each encoding a different relational structure from physical to abstract.
> 
> **Contribution**: A well-structured representational map sits between the two extremes of neural tuning: highly redundant and highly diverse and requires correlation among tuning properties. Maximal decorrelation, the naive optimum for decoding, collapses the map's relational structure. The representational maps can be useful for clinical conditions where map distortion through neural dropout or aberrant activity point directly to agnosia, delusion, and maladaptive association.

> [!citation]
> Noda T, Aschauer DF, Chambers AR, Seiler JP-H and Rumpel S (2024) Representational maps in the brain: concepts, approaches, and applications. Front. Cell. Neurosci. 18:1366200. doi: 10.3389/fncel.2024.1366200
