Have you ever reached for the bottle of water on your desk while reading something on the screen?
Have you ever tapped the back of a rickshaw as it crosses close to your body, without actively computing anything?
How does a footballer subconsciously map the entire field of players to inform their passes?

These all share a common thread: Integration of a spectrum of information from as simple as a brush of air on the forearm to complex visual/auditory cues. The integrated whole is more granular than any of the senses. I believe the swiftness and preciseness of the following action is the product of this phenomenon. I want to unravel this phenomenon with future applications directed towards elevating neurorehabilitation; improving viewing experiences related to movies, games, and anime; improving brain-body connection training programs for athletes.

My approach to research and learning involves a mixture of:
- tracing a phenomenon across history to understand its evolution; and 
- following buried threads from the current landscape.

Making clinical diagnoses involves a similar exercise. Two things helped me tremendously when I hit a wall:
- talking through the problem with a team member or friend helps reorganize the entire information in a digestible manner, inducing new connections;
- distracting myself from the problem if time allows. I prefer to game as a distraction.
