## Entorhinal-Hippocampal System Cell Types

| Cell Type                        | Location                                                          | Key Function                                                                                                                                                          |
| :------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Place cells**                  | *Hippocampus*                                                     | Fire for specific spatial locations; population represents entire environment; stable across days/weeks; remap in novel environments                                  |
| **Head direction cells**         | *Presubiculum, entorhinal cortex, retrosplenial cortex, thalamus* | Fire when animal faces a specific direction; independent of position or behaviour; coherently realign between environments                                            |
| **Grid cells**                   | *Medial entorhinal cortex (mEC)*                                  | Fire in repeating hexagonal grid across environment; grid patterns maintain stable relationship on rotation; involved in path integration and self-motion calculation |
| **Interneurons**                 | *Throughout hippocampal formation*                                | Regulate timing of spatial network activity via theta rhythm                                                                                                          |
| **Extrahippocampal place cells** | *mEC, lateral septum, thalamus*                                   | Place-like spatial encoding outside hippocampus; lateral septum cells may support pattern completion                                                                  |
| **Boundary / Border cells**      | *Subiculum, mEC, claustrum, thalamus*                             | Fire when animal approaches environmental boundaries; direction- and distance-specific                                                                                |
| **Object cells**                 | *Anterior claustrum, lateral entorhinal cortex <br>(lEC)*         | Encode spatial location of objects; lEC cells maintain memory trace after object removal                                                                              |
| **Goal cells**                   | *Medial prefrontal cortex*                                        | Fire at or near spatial goal locations independent of reward site                                                                                                     |
| **Conjunctive cells**            | *mEC, presubiculum, retrosplenial cortex*                         | Encode combinations of location, direction, and speed simultaneously                                                                                                  |
| **Action-sensitive cells**       | *Striatum, posterior parietal cortex, retrosplenial cortex*       | Encode movement at specific locations; PPC transforms egocentric to allocentric reference frame                                                                       |

## Beyond the Big Three

![[neural_basis_cognitive_map_storage_dark.svg]]\
*On the surface, this paper is about various cells involved in the representation of space in the entorhinal-hippocampal system. But at the core, it's really about providing more puzzle pieces beyond the "big three" cell types in order to prompt exploration of connection between the entorhinal/hippocampal system and spatial representations throughout the entire brain.*  
Tolman called the "internal representation of space" a *cognitive map*.  
The particular action of collating and presenting the above information felt like the authors were trying to clear the fog around the central puzzle. Place, Head Direction and Grid cell discoveries not only found the puzzle pieces for Tolman's definition of *cognitive map* but also built a framework to explain the visuo-spatial storage function of the entorhinal-hippocampal system. The authors successfully provided several puzzle pieces beyond these and prompted further exploration.  
I chose to capture this by an almost complete puzzle made up of the "big three" cell type pieces. This underscores the importance of the Nobel Prize-winning work in this area. The lesser-known cell types are scattered around the puzzle waiting to be brought into the framework. Tolman's definition: **Internal Representation of Space** lives in the surrounding fog.  
The paper prompted me towards a gap: How do all the cell types described in the paper connect to spatially modulated cells in the neocortex, particularly the cells which receive multimodal inputs along the "where" pathway? Is the map constructed in the hippocampus or in the neocortex?  


> [!synthesis]
> **Claim**: In order to realize the concept of internal spatial representation, expansion beyond the "big three" cell types is necessary.
> 
> **Contribution**: A systematic survey of lesser-known spatially modulated cell types: interneurons, boundary cells, object cells, goal cells, conjunctive cells, and action-sensitive cells.
