After reading <a href="Landscape/@Epstein_2019/@Epstein_2019" class="wikilink">Scene Perception in the Human Brain</a>, open questions for me were the following: Which neocortical areas have multimodal representations of senses and have spatially modulated cells? How do the representations in such cells compare with representations of space in areas such as PPA, MPA and OPA and the phylogenetically preserved entorhinal-hippocampal system? My search led me to a body of research which has been actively investigating the same question. The core questions in this body of research include:

- properties of “Spatial View Cells” which have been identified in non-human primates (NHP) and humans distinct from place cell-like cells.
- eye-movement modulation of place fields in primates
- effects of gaze on cognitive maps ie. “visual” cognitive maps

This paper was chosen because it addressed one of my open questions: comparison between a neocortical area with spatially modulated cells and hippocampus.
\## Behavioral Setup and Rationale

Several references provide the rationale for choosing a virtual-reality based 3D maze built using a 3D projector and viewed by the monkeys through shutter glasses which simulate the three-dimensional nature. Monkeys were head restrained and navigated through the star-shaped maze along preset paths using joysticks. The key rationale points include:

- NHP and rodents are capable of navigating in a virtual space most likely because optic flow information can simulate the environment along with visual processing of the scene.
- Primate hippocampus (HPC) has been shown to encode a map of the visual space based on what the animal looks at.
- Posterior parietal cortex (PPC), especially the intraparietal areas (VIP, LIP) is known to perform computations necessary for optic flow variables, motions of stimuli, objects and self based on saccades and fixations. These support the representation of the visual space.
- Anatomical connections exist between these two areas directly and via poly-synaptic pathways going through retrosplenial, posterior cingulate or parahippocampal cortices to enter the entorhinal-hippocampal system.

## Primate PPC and HPC organize space distinctly

<img src="ppc-plus-hpc_dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="ppc-plus-hpc_dark.svg" />\
*On the surface, this is about view and place modulations of neurons in PPC and HPC. But at the core, it’s really about how primate PPC and HPC neurons together build a cognitive map of a virtual space based on foveation of landmarks with PPC neurons linked to sensorimotor aspects of the task oriented towards the goal and HPC neurons showing task-driven processing post-reward.*\
The major finding of this paper can be described by the above metaphor. I chose to represent HPC cells as the eyes of a person trying to shoot an archery target. Homogeneously scanning the whole environment which includes the target, HPC cells provide the “global internal map of the space”. In order to shoot the target, the hands and the bow need to be oriented towards the task, thus representing the task-context and content properties of the PPC cells. The dynamic nature of this action matches the dynamic recruitment of cells with varied nature and degrees of modulation as shown in the study. The focus on the target (landmark) is deliberate to signify the anticipatory properties discovered. The unique property of HPC cells post-reward is shown as the archer studying his result, thus preserving the task-context property of these cells. The “studying” action is deliberate to show the possible role of this property in learning.

I am left with the following open questions beyond the future directions suggested by the authors:

- Could the asymmetric activity of neurons with lower stability of spatial modulation be due to the virtual nature of the task where the subject is completely stationary throughout the task? Has similar activity been observed in naturalistic settings?
- How do both areas communicate with each other? Is the sensorimotor saliency map the limit of the PPC cells?
- Is the cognitive map in HPC derived piecewise from the salient maps of the visual space in the PPC? This mechanistically connects to <a href="Landscape/@Noppeney_2021/@Noppeney_2021" class="wikilink">Perceptual Inference, Learning, and Attention in a Multisensory World</a>.

> [!synthesis]
> **Claim**: Primate PPC and HPC neurons together build a cognitive map of a virtual space based on foveation of landmarks with PPC neurons linked to sensorimotor aspects of the task oriented towards the goal and HPC neurons showing task-driven processing post-reward.
>
> **Contribution**: Vericel et al. recorded primate PPC and HPC neurons in a virtual-reality-based 3D navigation task and defined these cells’ properties based on both virtual position and view, which led to the emergence of “landmark” and “path” cells.
