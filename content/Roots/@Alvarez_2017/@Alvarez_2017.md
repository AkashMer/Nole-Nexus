## How do dissolved substances diffuse in a liquid solvent?

<img src="ficks-diffusion-dark.svg" class="wikilink" width="600" />

In 1855, [Adolf Fick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eugen_Fick) defined and experimentally verified a law for the diffusion of salts in liquid ([Fick, 1855](#ref-fick_1855)) by connecting the phenomenon to heat ([Fourier’s law of heat conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conduction#Fourier's_law)) and electrical conduction ([Ohm’s law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm's_law)). Fick used his law to give a mechanical explanation for [Graham’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Graham_(chemist)) extensive investigation on the diffusion of salts in water and diffusion of salt solutions through porous partitions, for example, a muslin cloth. During this time, ions and membranes were not yet part of the picture.

## [Nernst-Planck (NP) Equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nernst%E2%80%93Planck_equation)

Around 1888, Nernst extended Fick’s diffusion law for ions in a solution ([Nernst, 1888](#ref-nernst_1888)). The major difference was a term which represented the force exerted by the electrical field generated by charges on ions. Nernst made an assumption about the electroneutrality of ionic salt solutions to derive his equation. Planck solidified this assumption by defining the conditions under which it holds true ([Planck, 1890](#ref-planck_1890a)).

## Why do biological specimens show selectivity to electrical current direction?

<img src="ghk-equation-dark.svg" class="wikilink" width="550" />

The NP equation could not be solved easily, even under steady-state conditions since it was a multi-variable differential equation (DE). [David E. Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Goldman) assumed that the electrical field remains constant across the membrane, turning the equation into a simple first-order DE.

Hodgkin & Katz applied the Goldman equation to compute changes in conductance of the individual ions during the action potential. This allowed Hodgkin & Katz to provide a mechanistic explanation for the Hodgkin-Huxley finding of the <a href="Roots/bernstein-to-overshoot/bernstein-to-overshoot" class="wikilink">"overshoot"</a>, which contradicted <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006" class="wikilink">Bernstein's electrochemical Membrantheorie</a>.

> [!synthesis]
> **Claim**: Goldman’s constant-field equation helped provide a physical/mathematical basis for bioelectric phenomena in membranes by making the Nernst–Planck equation of ion diffusion solvable, so that it could be applied.
>
> **Contribution**: Authors reconstruct the lineage of the GHK equation as part of the centenary issue, providing clear derivations, assumptions and failure points along the way.
