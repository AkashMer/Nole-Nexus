## Non-Spiritual Physiology

<img src="non-spiritual-physiology_dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="non-spiritual-physiology_dark.svg" />\
*On the surface, this chapter traces the path from antiquity to fluid-to-solid paradigm shift. But at the core, it’s really about how co-occurrence of anatomical and functional evidence of fibers became the first domino for the fluid-to-solid paradigm shift.*\
Open questions from <a href="Roots/@Rocca_2003/@Rocca_2003" class="wikilink">Galen</a> about the “discharging faculty” of gallbladder and the intrinsic capacity of the muscle to retract were revisited during the [17th century Scientific Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution). This period was marked by a shift to mechanistic models such as <a href="Roots/@Lokhorst_2021/@Lokhorst_2021" class="wikilink">Descartes' bellows-plus-pineal-gland model</a> and empirical inquiry, focusing on experimentation and observation.\
I chose to capture the co-occurrence of the anatomical and the experimental evidence of fibers by showing the instruments through which the evidence was obtained: the microscope and the gall bladder. Francis Glisson’s **irritability** concept built using this connection was extended and applied to muscles by Giorgio Baglivi. Something interesting had happened here, which is conveyed by the presence of both the animal spirits and the fiber in the model: Another concept had entered the “arena” alongside the animal spirits.

## Despirited Physiology
<img src="despirited_physiology_dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="despirited_physiology_dark.svg" />\
*On the surface, this chapter is about historic retelling of spiritual physiology at a time when solids have entered the arena. But at the core, it’s really about recurrent pattern of camouflaging uncertainty to fit the era’s keystone belief so the novel idea survives until a crack appears.*\
The end of 17th century and major part of 18th century was guided by Herman Boerhaave and his students. Boerhaave described human anatomy to be composed of fluids and solid parts with “minimal fibers” as the ultimate unit, but maintained that all motions proceed from fluids. This belief was shared by many of his students. The following period was marked by fibrous structure descriptions, models to explain **irritability** which sometimes included black boxes such as *“enormôn”* or the *active sentient principle/forces* and theories which described mental faculties as a product of organic changes in the brain. Albrecht von Haller, one such student along with Johann Zimmermann described a theory for irritability backed by experiments on living animals. Hallerian theory retained that the contractile power was derived from nerves and therefore the soul. Inspired by his mentor, Felice Fontana gave the laws of irritability and called for experimentation to determine the properties of the animal spirits with the goal of dismissing it from physiology entirely.\
I chose to capture this back and forth as a trojan horse which carried the uncertainty of each theory inside it, but was wearing the facade of keystone beliefs of the time. As more and more uncertainty gathered along this winding road to despirited physiology, the keystone belief cracked under the weight of growing gaps in the animal spirits physiology. Through these gaps, the uncertainties could now be explored without this framework.\
A similar pattern occurred for <a href="Roots/@Rocca_2003/@Rocca_2003#^e5e447" class="wikilink">hegemonic concept development</a> during Galen’s time.  

## The Electrical Zeitgiest
<img src="electrical_world_dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="electrical_world_dark.svg" />\
*On the surface, this chapter is about rapid rise of electricity. But at the core, it’s really about how the invisible forces Zeitgeist provided a climate for the expanding science of electricity to permeate nerve and muscle physiology.*\
Parallel to the above advances and the discovery of various invisible forces such as Gilbert’s electricity, Newton’s gravity, Mesmer’s animal magnetism, and gases of the Charliéres (inflammable hydrogen) and Montgolfières (hot air), the science of electricity was also growing. The understanding of making, detecting and storing electricity along with Stephen Gray’s “suspended electrified boy” inspired many proponents such as Abbé Jean-Antoine Nollet, Benjamin Franklin and Robert Turner. This led to the consolidation and expansion of the different types of electricity into every facet it could find. The electrical fishes provided the ideal substrate which allowed electricity to enter the realm of nerve and muscle physiology.\
I chose to capture this by comparing the science of electricity to a cloud which feeds on small clouds which represent small advances made by the proponents. To me, this is reminiscent of how Nami uses Clima-Tact to feed Zeus in the hit anime series [One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece_(1999_TV_series)). The Electrical *Zeitgeist* cloud’s thunder discharge electrified the animal spirits in the model.\
\
The turning point for many opponents which included [Haller](#dispirited-physiology) was Walsh’s experiment on a live eel which showed a spark skipping across a cut wire in darkness.\
Three questions could be asked now:

1.  How electricity could be confined within nerves without diffusing through surrounding tissue?
2.  Whether fish electricity is identical to atmospheric and machine electricity or subtly different?
3.  Whether common animals like frogs, horses, humans also run on electricity too subtle to detect?

## Animal Electricity & Birth of Modern Neurophysiology

<img src="animal_electricity_dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="animal_electricity_dark.svg" />\
*On the surface, this chapter gives the timeline for the birth of neurophysiology as we know it today. But at the core, it’s really about how a rigorous physicochemical approach transformed the electrical animal spirits and their flow nature into a potential across the cell membrane.*\
Towards the end of 18th century, Luigi Galvani further expanded the reach of the Electrical Zeitgeist to all animals beyond the electrical fishes by using *“prepared frogs”* and a program of experiments spanning 11 years. These experiments showed replicable contractions from the touch of a metal and recovery of the fatigue from repetitive stimulation. The effects were greater when a nerve was stimulated. Galvani proposed a biological Leyden jar model and the electricity was prepared by the brain. Alessandro Volta opposed the idea of general animal electricity beyond electrical fishes and attributed the metals as the source of electricity. Galvani designed new experiments which showed contractions in the absence of metal and any heterogenous material.\
The undeniable proof for animal electricity was provided by Italians and Germans throughout the 19th century. In addition, the source of animal electricity was shifted to the nerve or muscle itself. I chose to capture the contributions of each scientist as a recursive puzzle piece, which answered the open questions left by a prior experiment, thus allowing to ask new questions. The notable absence of *animal spirits* beyond the 1st piece signifies the paradigm shift which occurred in the background because animal electricity was consistently better at answering the open questions. The position of the pieces is also deliberate showing the internal conceptual connections between different discoveries.\
Julius Bernstein’s *electrochemical Membrantheorie* was the culmination of Galvani’s animal electricity phenomenon and left two open questions:

1.  What was the structure of the semi-permeable membrane?
2.  What was the role of $Na^+$?

> [!synthesis]
> **Claim**: The paradigm shift from the animal spirits doctrine to modern neurophysiology was gradual over two millennia because two things had to be in place: dislodgement of animal spirits and a credible alternative propelled by the electricity Zeitgeist.
>
> **Contribution**: Smith et al. assemble a start-to-finish historical telling of this paradigm shift, exposing resistances, half-repairs, and competing agendas.
