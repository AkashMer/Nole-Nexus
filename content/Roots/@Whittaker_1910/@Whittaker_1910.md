<a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006" class="wikilink">Bernstein's</a> paradigm-shifting *electrochemical Membrantheorie* passed through the hands of [Rudolf Höber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_H%C3%B6ber), [Kenneth Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Stewart_Cole) and many more before the Hodgkin-Huxley model of a neuron was presented in the 1950s. The major shift around the time of Bernstein’s work was that AC current was now readily available in homes and laboratories. In order to understand the post-Bernstein era of neurophysiological research, I felt that getting a basic understanding of different electrical theories which were used to probe and model the bioelectric phenomena of living tissue was paramount. This book was chosen specifically because it is contemporaneous with Höber’s work (1910). This allowed me to have a faithful account of electrical theory as understood during this period.
