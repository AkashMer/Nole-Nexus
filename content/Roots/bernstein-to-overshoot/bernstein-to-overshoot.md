Title: From Membrane Models to the Overshoot
created: 2026-08-29
modified: 2026-08-29
---

In 1902, <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006" class="wikilink">Bernstein's</a> model placed the cell membrane as a key structure necessary for tissue excitability. Around the time, the <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006#Birth of the Cell Membrane" class="wikilink">concept of the cell membrane itself was not yet settled</a>. The years leading up to Hodgkin & Huxley’s rediscovery of the famous “overshoot” were filled with research which quantitatively defined the properties of the membrane. During the same time period, the first structural models of the membrane were defined. Thus, a brief description of these models is required to understand the landscape during which [Kenneth Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Stewart_Cole) was working on excitability.

## First Membrane Models

<img src="first-membrane-models-dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="mindmap" />\
Reproduced from Lombard (2014), published in *Biology Direct* under a [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) license ([Lombard, 2014](#ref-Lombard_2014)).\
The lipoid nature along with the presence of non-lipid compounds in the membrane was already known even before the confirmation under an electron microscope. Various mechanisms behind the asymmetry of ion distributions across a membrane hinted at an energy-consuming process to maintain this asymmetry. The sandwich model of the membrane was predominant during these years, but “mosaic models” were already described albeit in a difference sense than the modern fluid mosaic model. Thus, the membrane structure was not yet solved during this time period.

## Tissue Excitability

<img src="hober-to-cole-to-overshoot-dark.svg" class="wikilink" alt="hober-to-cole-to-overshoot-dark.svg" />\
*On the surface this is about the journey from membrane models to the overshoot. But at the core, it’s really about how different eyeglasses to study a phenomenon and simultaneous appearance of a new substrate scoped out Bernstein’s membrane theory quantitatively, revealing the next layer to peel: potential drop beyond zero.*\
I chose to represent the membrane theory of excitability as a cube which was being studied through different instruments and methods (detective’s glasses) during this time period. Hober used AC electricity to separate the RBC cell interior from the membrane using resistivity changes at different frequencies of the alternating current ([Cole, 1968](#ref-Cole_1968)). The advent of AC current as easily available technology in a lab was not drastic and ties back to <a href="Roots/@Whittaker_1910/@Whittaker_1910" class="wikilink">how electricity evolved over the 19th century</a> and the <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006#Birth of Modern Physical Chemistry and Electrochemistry" class="wikilink">electrochemical research</a> by [Friedrich Kohlrausch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Kohlrausch_(physicist)) and [Walther Nernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Nernst). Cole & Curtis the first ones who quantitatively described the excitability of cell membranes by modelling it as a resistor-capacitor circuit based on the axon cable theory. This was non-trivial since now the membrane properties were linked to measurable quantities like impedance and capacitance ([Catacuzzeno et al., 2024](#ref-Catacuzzeno_2024)).\
The breakthrough which allowed the measurement of internal potential of a cell and the voltage dependence of permeability of membrane is attributed to John Z. Young’s introduction of the squid giant axon (1 mm in diameter) ([Carmeliet, 2019](#ref-Carmeliet_2019)). In 1939, Cole & Curtis simultaneously measured impedance and change in membrane potential using extracellular electrodes in a single axon. They quantitatively proved the breakdown of impermeability by showing a 40-fold decrease in resistance during excitation compared to rest. They also linked the time course of the movement of ions (the current) and the change in potential to the change in resistance of the membrane ([Catacuzzeno et al., 2024](#ref-Catacuzzeno_2024)). Thus, all the results were consistent with <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006#Membrane Theory (1902)" class="wikilink">Bernstein's theory of excitability</a>. But they only measured the extracellular potential and were not able to provide a quantitative measure of the action potential.\
At the same time across the ocean, Hodgkin & Huxley were trying to solve the problem of inserting the electrode into the interior of the squid giant axon without scraping the surface. Huxley came up with the solution by using a combination of two mirrors which showed the top and side views of the insertion process side by side ([Carmeliet, 2019](#ref-Carmeliet_2019)). They provided the first quantitative recordings of the action potential. These recordings showed that the potential does not drop to zero as predicted by <a href="Roots/@Seyfarth_2006/@Seyfarth_2006#Membrane Theory (1902)" class="wikilink">Bernstein's theory</a>, but goes beyond it to negative 40-50 mV ([Catacuzzeno et al., 2024](#ref-Catacuzzeno_2024)). They were able to peel the layer and expose the next frontier as shown in the above visual. Sadly, they were not able to continue with the research immediately due to the advent of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) on September 1, 1939.
